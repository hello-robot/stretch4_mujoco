# Stretch 4 MJCF and URDF

The MJCF is now dynamically generated on-the-fly from the `stretch4_urdf` package using `urdf2mjcf` and programmatic DOM manipulations.

When the simulator is initialized, `get_robot_xml_path()` dynamically invokes `mjcf_generator.py` to convert the appropriate URDF into an MJCF and applies custom modifications required by Mujoco.

## Programmatic Transformations Applied
The `mjcf_generator.py` applies the following changes to the raw `urdf2mjcf` output:

1. Removes all tags except `asset` and `worldbody`.
2. Removes specific tags from worldbody (e.g., `light`, `camera`, `ground` plane).
3. Converts the URDF's rolling wheels into omniwheels (`left_wheel_link`, `back_wheel_link`, `right_wheel_link`). Placement and inertials are left exactly as derived from the URDF; only the omnidirectional contact model (the `omniwheel_collision` capsule) and the joint names the actuators drive are overridden. Note the URDF's axles point opposite to what the `H0` drive kinematics assumes, which is what the negative `wheelN_polarity` values in `config.py` compensate for.
4. Encapsulates lidar and camera geoms with dedicated bodies and includes `hemisphere_lidar_cameras_right/left.xml`, and adds a `line_sensor_N` site at the emitter of each of the six base line sensors for ray casting.
5. Replaces `link_mast` geometry with appropriate `collision` and `visualgeom` classes.
6. Renames the root body to `base_link` and adds an `imu` site.
7. Encapsulates gripper fingers into `link_gripper_slider` (if present, e.g. for sg4 tool).
8. Adjusts `class="visualgeom"` and adds `class="rubber"` to gripper fingers.
9. Modifies wrist roll to include `gripper_cameras.xml`.
10. Adds `gravcomp="1"` to lift, arm, and wrist components.
11. Maps joints to appropriate classes defined in `defaults.xml` (`lift_stretch4`, `telescope`, `wrist_yaw_stretch4`, etc.).
12. On the SG4 tool, replaces the rigid fingers with the calibrated, compliant fingers of the real gripper (`sg4_tool/`, see below).

## SG4 compliant fingers

`sg4_tool/compliant_fingers.py` rebuilds the SG4's fingers so the fingertips follow the robot's measured path and the fingers give under load:

- `gripper_finger_{left,right}_joint` keep their names, classes and actuators. Their range is `-0.16..0.5`: 0 is closed (the cups meet), 0.5 fully open, and below 0 squeezes. The servo maps as `pos_pct = 600 * q`.
- Each fingertip rides x, y and yaw joints tied to a follower joint by the fingertip calibration's cubics (`stretch4_compliant_gripper`'s `latest_model_planar.yaml` for robot 4026, embedded in `sg4_tool/calibration_4026.py`). The motor pulls the follower through a spring tendon (`FLEX_K`), so blocked fingertips stop while the motor still reaches its command; the spring's stretch is the grip force.
- The steel strips are hinge chains following the render rig's shapes (`sg4_tool/sg4_spring_shapes.json`), pinned to the fingertips. They collide with the world, so they push objects, but not with the gripper body or fingertips. Their fit takes a few seconds and is cached in `autogenerated_sg4_spring_design.json`.
- The equalities, tendons and the cup-on-cup contact pair are appended to `autogenerated_contact.xml`.

`FLEX_K`, the `fingertip_compliance` class, the cup friction and `calibration.BAND_GIVE` are estimates until measured on a robot. `calibration.grasp_reach(radius)` gives where an object's centre should be for a centred grasp, as the tips pull back about 34 mm toward the wrist as they close.

These steps have been fully automated and replace the previously documented manual processes.
